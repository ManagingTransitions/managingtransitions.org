In brief

I advise on programme handover and aid-dependency exits. The advice draws on field programme management in The Gambia, Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania, and on doctoral research into who holds decisions and money in aid partnerships. This page sets out what I do, the evidence behind it and how to work with me.

What I do

Managing Transitions is my advisory practice. The work usually takes one of four forms, as a short piece of advice, a review or a retained engagement.

Aid-dependency exit design

You're handing a programme or network to partners. I work out what can transfer, in what order, and what you stay liable for.

Outcomes and evidence architecture

Turning activity data into outcomes a donor will still trust after you've left.

Organisational and governance advisory

An independent read of structures, boards and partnerships while roles and power are changing hands.

Political economy and localisation analysis

Who holds the decisions and the money in a partnership, and what that means for your plan.

What UK NGOs say about handovers

For the 2026 UK INGO Transition Reporting Review I read what 23 UK international NGOs have published about handing programmes over. Five put a figure against money moving to partners. Six gave a handover timetable separate from their own redundancy process. Four said how anyone would know if the transition worked. Every score traces back to a quotation from the organisation's own documents.

What I mean by localisation

Too much of what gets called localisation is administrative relocation dressed up as empowerment. The reporting burden moves south while the funding rules and the veto stay where they were. I only call something local ownership if the power moved with the paperwork, and I don't treat local actors as passive until a donor grants them some.

Transition Exposure Review

If you're closing an office, consolidating into hubs or handing a programme to partners, the reduction is usually planned and the transfer usually isn't. The review sets out:

  • which functions can transfer within twelve months, and which cannot
  • the single points of failure that sit with you: banking, compliance, certification, the donor relationship
  • residual liability after handover: employment, safeguarding, registration, assets, records
  • what a funder would need to see before accepting the plan
  • a costed sequence

The shape of a review gets worked out in a first conversation. If a review isn't the right thing for you, I'll say so on the call and you'll have lost half an hour.
