For funders

Three questions to ask before you fund a transition

If a grant involves an organisation closing an office, handing a programme to partners or shrinking its footprint, these are the three things its published documents most often leave out. They can be asked when the condition is written, they cost nothing, and the answers are checkable later.

1What money moves to partners?

Five of 23 UK international NGOs in the 2026 review put a figure against it.

2On what timetable?

Six of 23 give a handover timetable separate from their own redundancy or closure timetable.

3How will anyone know it held?

Four of 23 state any measure of whether the transition worked.

A minimum standard

A transition statement should say what work is moving and to whom, what resource moves with it, on what timetable that is separate from the organisation’s own, what the departing organisation stays responsible for afterwards, and how anyone will know it held. It is not a high bar, and no one is currently asked to meet it.

Why in writing

Much of the handing over probably happens in conversations, favours and side agreements. Very little of it is written anywhere a partner, a funder or a trustee could point to. Care that stays unwritten protects the people who were in the room and nobody after them. A written statement is the part of a handover that outlasts the staff who arranged it.

Using this

Copy the three questions and the standard into your conditions. The study, the dataset and the codebook are open under CC BY 4.0. If you would rather have an applicant’s transition tested before you fund it, that is what the Transition Exposure Review is for.

Source: UK INGO Transition Reporting Review 2026, Matt Thomson, 23 organisations coded from their own published documents.
